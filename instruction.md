The beck at sedgemere is surveyed a spot at a time: a surveyor takes eight readings, and the water level at that spot is written down afterwards. We have four thousand past surveys with their levels, and we need a book that can give the level for a spot from its readings alone.

/app/docs/BRIEF.md describes the readings, the two data files, and exactly how the book is invoked and what it must write. In short, /app/data/train.csv holds four thousand surveys with their levels, /app/data/dev.csv holds eight thousand more without them alongside /app/data/dev.levels so you can measure yourself honestly, and the book is C++ under /app/src, built with "g++ -O2 -std=c++17", and is run as "sedgemere readings.csv levels.txt", writing one level per survey in the order it received them.

Not every reading carries signal, and the ones that do are not all separate: two of them do nothing apart and a great deal together, and which two they are is not the same for every survey, because one of the gates decides it. A book that weighs each reading on its own and adds the weights up gets a fair way and then stops well short, and so does one that looks for a single combination across all the surveys at once.

There is a further thing, and it is about the levels rather than the readings. A level is usually read to within a hand's breadth, but roughly one survey in six is a spike, and a spike is always upward, never downward. You are scored on the plain distance between the level you give and the level held, counting a miss either way the same. What one-sided spikes do to a fit, and what follows for a book scored that way, is yours to work out.

Nothing beyond the C++ standard library is available and there is no network, so the fitting is yours to write rather than to import. One run must finish within five minutes. Python is on the box if you want it for looking at the data, but the book itself has to be the C++ program; if you run a long experiment while you work, start it in the background so the shell stays yours.

You are judged on levels, not on any file you write about them. Your book is handed surveys drawn the same way as the training set, whose levels it has never seen, and scored on how far its levels sit from the true ones on average; it must come within 1.35 on each of several fresh draws, so a book tuned to one particular sample will not pass. Giving every spot the middle of the training levels misses by about 2.8, and the best a book could do is about 1.25. The book shipped at /app/src/sedgemere.cpp gives every spot the same level; replace it.
