20 - THE RECORD                                   C029 - C070
==============================================================

C029  THE RECORD IS READ TOP LINE FIRST, one step at a time, and the closing sheet is settled only
      after the last step has been read. The order of the lines is the whole of the history: the
      same board standing before a close and standing after it settles two different sheets.
C030  Every line of the record opens with a step number and then the KIND of the step. The number is
      there for reading and quoting only. The referee weighs the steps in the ORDER THE LINES STAND
      and never sorts, groups or renumbers them.
C031  THERE ARE SIX KINDS OF STEP AND NO OTHERS: seat, board, bye, concede, strike and close. The
      close is ruled in 40-the-close.txt; the other five are ruled here.
C032  A STEP NAMING A BERTH THE BRACKET DOES NOT NAME reaches nothing. It is read, it is passed
      over, no house moves, no count moves, and no berth is made.
C033  A STEP NAMING A HOUSE THE ROLL DOES NOT NAME reaches nothing, in the same way and for the same
      reason.
C034  A STEP THAT REACHES NOTHING is still part of the record. It takes no line on the closing
      sheet, it is counted in no column of any line, and it is counted on the head line in none of
      boards, byes or concedes (C149).
C035  NAMING A BERTH. Any berth THE BRACKET NAMES that a step names is NAMED by that step, whether
      the step reaches anything or not. This is the only thing a step that reaches nothing does
      leave behind, and it is what separates dark.named from dark.silent (C135). A berth the bracket
      does not name is not named by anything, because it is not a berth.
C036  seat. The step reads `seat` and then a HOUSE and then a BERTH, in that order.
C037  Where the step reaches, the house is seated in the berth: it stands in that berth from this
      step, the berth seats it, and the berth counts one more house seated for the purpose of
      held.long and held.own (C128).
C038  A seat REACHES NOTHING where the house named is not on the roll.
C039  A seat REACHES NOTHING where the berth named is not in the bracket.
C040  A seat REACHES NOTHING where the house named is already out of the crown, whether it was
      struck, cut or felled.
C041  A seat REACHES NOTHING where the house named already stands in a berth, that berth being the
      one named or any other.
C042  A seat REACHES NOTHING where the berth named already seats a house, that house being the one
      named or any other.
C043  A seat that reaches nothing still NAMES its berth (C035), so a berth nothing else ever touched
      reads dark.named and not dark.silent.
C044  board. The step reads `board` and then a BERTH and then a BERTH and then two counts of HANDS,
      the first count belonging to the first berth named and the second to the second.
C045  A board REACHES NOTHING unless BOTH berths named seat a house that is still in the crown. A
      berth seating nobody, and a berth whose house has been struck, cut or felled, both stop it.
C046  A board REACHES NOTHING where it names THE SAME BERTH TWICE, because a house never meets
      itself (C025).
C047  Where the board reaches, the house taking THE MORE HANDS takes the board.
C048  WHERE BOTH TOOK THE SAME HANDS the board falls to the house with the WORSE OPENING SEED, that
      is, the higher seed number. It never falls to the better seed and it is never left undecided.
C049  A BOARD ON WHICH NEITHER SIDE TOOK A HAND AT ALL is weighed exactly like any other board of
      equal hands: it falls to the worse opening seed under C048, and both sides enter it with
      nothing in their hand columns.
C050  A board that is taken enters a WIN for the house that took it and a LOSS for the house that
      did not.
C051  A board enters HANDS for both houses. Each house takes the hands it took and gives the hands
      the other took: the winner's taken rises by the winner's hands and its given by the loser's,
      and the loser's taken rises by the loser's hands and its given by the winner's.
C052  A board moves both BERTHS it names, whoever was sitting in them: each berth counts one more
      board, and each berth's hands rise by the hands taken AT THAT BERTH.
C053  THE MEETING. A board makes a MEETING FOR BOTH HOUSES: each has met the other. A house that
      meets the same house at three boards has met it three times, and every one of those meetings
      is counted separately for strength (C080).
C054  A board that reaches nothing enters no win, no loss, no hand and no meeting, and moves neither
      berth's board count nor either berth's hands. The berths are still NAMED (C035).
C055  A board that reaches sets two ROUND MARKS: both houses STOOD IN A BOARD in the round now
      running, and the house that did not take it LOST A BOARD in the round now running. What those
      marks decide is ruled at C107 through C113.
C056  bye. The step reads `bye` and CARRIES NO ARGUMENT AT ALL: no house, no berth and no count. The
      standing decides where it falls.
C057  A BYE FALLS TO THE LOWEST HOUSE STILL SEATED, that is, the last house of the standing taken at
      this step over every house standing in a berth and still in the crown (C089). It never falls
      to the highest, it never falls by berth and it never falls by seed.
C058  A bye enters ONE WIN for the house it falls on, toward the standing, and nothing else whatever.
C059  A bye enters NO BOARD, NO HANDS AND NO MEETING. The berth the house stands in counts no board
      for it and takes no hands from it, and the house meets nobody, so a bye lifts a house without
      lifting the strength of anybody at all.
C060  A bye WHERE NO HOUSE IS SEATED reaches nothing: it is recorded, it falls on nobody, and it is
      counted in no column.
C061  A bye MAY FALL ON A HOUSE THAT HAS ALREADY DRAWN ONE, in this round or in any earlier round.
      Nothing forbids it and both wins count.
C062  A bye sets ONE ROUND MARK: the house it falls on STOOD IN A BOARD in the round now running,
      although no board was played. It sets no losing mark and no giving mark.
C063  concede. The step reads `concede` and then a BERTH and then a BERTH. THE FIRST BERTH NAMED IS
      THE GIVER: the house standing there gives the board up to the house standing in the second.
C064  A concession enters a LOSS FOR THE GIVER and NO WIN FOR THE OTHER. This is the only ruling in
      these laws where a board is settled and one side gains nothing by it.
C065  A concession's MEETING IS ONE-SIDED: the giver has met the other, and the other has met
      nobody. So the giver's later wins raise the other house's strength by nothing, while the
      other's later wins raise the giver's strength as any meeting does.
C066  A concession enters NO HANDS for either side, and moves neither house's taken or given. It
      does count ONE BOARD AT EACH of the two berths it names, and it moves neither berth's hands.
C067  A concession REACHES NOTHING where either berth seats nobody, where either berth's house is
      out of the crown, or where it names THE SAME BERTH TWICE.
C068  A concession that reaches sets THREE ROUND MARKS: both houses STOOD IN A BOARD in the round
      now running, the giver LOST A BOARD in it, and the giver GAVE A BOARD UP in it. The last of
      the three is what separates felled.given from felled.board (C109).
C069  strike. The step reads `strike` and then a HOUSE. The house comes OFF THE ROLL AT ONCE: it is
      out of the crown from this step, it stands in no berth from this step, and no later step of
      any kind reaches it.
C070  A STRUCK HOUSE'S BOARDS STAND. Every win it handed out still counts to the house that took it,
      every loss it handed out still counts, every hand on either side stands, and every meeting it
      made stands, so its own wins go on feeding the strength of every house it met (C085). A strike
      naming a house already out, or a house not on the roll, is recorded and changes nothing at
      all: it does not re-state the house and does not disturb its ruling.
